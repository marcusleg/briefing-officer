# Briefing Officer — Product Requirements

Status: draft, documents the product as built in version 0.15.0. Last updated:
2026-09-13.

This is a lean, as-built PRD. It records what the application already does so
that later work has a shared baseline. The section layout follows the "Problem
Alignment / Solution Alignment" split of Kevin Yien's PRD template (the full
template recommended by Lenny's Newsletter), so it can grow into a full PRD by
adding sections rather than rewriting. See "Extending this document" at the end.

## 1. Overview

Briefing Officer is a self-hosted, multi-user news reader for RSS and Atom
feeds. Its centre is AI summarisation at two depths: a short lead on every
article that is meant to be sufficient on its own, and a longer bullet-point
summary on request for quick digestion. The lead is not a teaser. It is written
so that a reader can decide they know enough and move on without opening
anything. Alongside this, AI keeps articles the reader would not care about out
of their inbox.

It runs as a single container with a SQLite database, is deployable with the
included Helm chart, and works with OpenAI, Azure OpenAI, Anthropic, or any
OpenAI-compatible API.

## 2. Problem and users

People who follow many feeds spend most of their reading time triaging: opening
articles only to find they are irrelevant, duplicated, or sponsored. Existing
readers show headlines and excerpts, which is not enough to triage well, and
they offer no way to say "less of this" that actually learns.

**Primary user:** an individual who self-hosts their own tools, follows tens of
feeds, and reads on both desktop and phone. Additional household or team members
may share the same instance with separate accounts.

Job stories:

- When new articles arrive, I want a one-paragraph lead for each that tells me
  the substance, so I can decide I know enough and move on, or dig deeper.
- When I have five minutes, I want the key facts of an article, so I can get the
  substance without reading the whole piece.
- When my hands or eyes are busy, I want an article read aloud to me, so I can
  keep up with news while doing something else.
- When a feed keeps producing articles I do not care about, I want to tell the
  reader once, so it stops showing me similar articles.
- When I find something worth keeping, I want to park it or mark it, so I can
  find it again later.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

Goals:

- Let the reader finish with most articles from the lead alone. The lead
  informs; it does not tease.
- Keep the inbox to articles the reader actually wants to see.
- Work equally well on a phone and on a desktop, in light and dark mode.
- Stay simple to self-host: one container, one database file, one cron call.
- Let the operator choose their AI provider.

Non-goals (as of this version):

- Being a full-text reading app. Reading the article happens on the source site;
  Briefing Officer only summarises.
- Social features: sharing, commenting, or public profiles.
- Importing or exporting feed lists (no OPML support).
- Server-side text-to-speech. Audio briefings use the browser's own voices.
- Offline reading.

## 4. Key features (as built)

Ordered by importance to the core job of triaging news.

1. **Feed reading.** Subscribe to RSS and Atom feeds by URL, group them into
   categories, pause and resume automatic refresh per feed, and refresh a feed,
   a category, or everything on demand. The sidebar shows unread counts per
   feed.
2. **AI lead.** Every ingested article gets a lead of at most 80 words that says
   what it covers and why it matters. It is written to inform, not to spark
   interest: for many articles the lead is all the reader needs. It is shown on
   the article card together with author, relative date, and a reading-time
   estimate.
3. **AI text summary.** On request, a streamed bullet list of 5 to 12 key facts
   or takeaways for one article, for readers who want the substance without
   reading the whole piece.
4. **AI audio briefing (experimental).** On request, a roughly one-minute spoken
   script generated by AI and read aloud by the browser's speech voices, with
   play, pause, restart, adjustable speed, and a transcript that highlights the
   sentence being spoken.
5. **Article language.** Summaries are written in the language of the article,
   and the audio player warns when no matching voice is installed.
6. **AI inbox filtering.** Each feed has "interested in" and "not interested in"
   keyword lists. At ingest, the AI hides articles that do not match and records
   a one-sentence reason. Filtered articles stay reviewable in a Filtered view
   and can be restored.
7. **Not interested.** One click files an article as filtered and suggests three
   keywords at different breadths to add to the feed's filter, so the filter
   learns from the rejection. Every action can be undone from a toast.
8. **Triage actions.** Dismiss, restore, read later, star, open the original,
   open the comments link when the feed provides one, and bulk mark as read for
   all articles or those older than a chosen number of days or weeks.
9. **Views.** Home inbox across all feeds, per-feed and per-category views with
   an All / Unread / Filtered switch, Read Later, Starred, History of the last
   50 read articles, and full-text search over titles and article text.
10. **Keyboard navigation.** Move between articles and trigger summary, audio,
    visit, and dismiss from the keyboard.
11. **Dashboard.** Charts of unread articles by feed, daily new, read, and
    filtered articles, and daily AI token usage by model, over 7 days, 30 days,
    or 3 months.
12. **Accounts and multi-user.** Email and password sign-in and sign-up,
    optional generic OAuth / OIDC single sign-on, and fully separated data per
    user. The first user becomes admin. Later sign-ups wait for admin approval
    unless self-registration is enabled. Admins can disable, enable, and delete
    users.
13. **Responsive UI.** Light, dark, and system theme; collapsible sidebar;
    phone-friendly card layout.

## 5. Key flows

- **Daily triage.** Sign in, land on the home inbox, skim leads, dismiss or read
  later as you go, open a text or audio summary for the few articles that
  warrant it, open the original for the ones that warrant more.
- **Adding a feed.** Paste a URL in the sidebar form, optionally set a title and
  category, adjust the prefilled "not interested in" defaults, save. The feed is
  fetched and its articles get leads and filter decisions.
- **Teaching the filter.** Click "not interested" on an article, pick one of the
  suggested keywords or type your own, and the feed's filter updates. Review the
  Filtered view occasionally and restore anything caught wrongly.
- **Operator setup.** Run the container with a database path, one AI provider's
  credentials, an auth secret, a base URL, and a cron token. Schedule a call to
  the cron endpoint every 15 minutes.

Screenshots of the main screens live in `docs/screenshots/`.

## 6. Key logic and constraints

- Article state is a single status (unread, read later, read, filtered) plus an
  independent star flag. The state machine is documented in
  `docs/reference/article-status.md`.
- Articles older than 365 days are purged on each cron run, except starred and
  read-later articles.
- The lead and the filter decision come from one AI call per article at ingest.
  Text and audio summaries are generated on demand and streamed.
- The AI provider is chosen by environment variables; the first configured
  provider wins, in the order Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, OpenAI-compatible.
- All data is scoped by user. There is no sharing between accounts.
- The sign-up page is always reachable. With self-registration disabled, new
  accounts start disabled and an administrator enables them to approve. Sign-in
  to a disabled account tells the user that approval is pending.
- Storage is SQLite. Refresh is driven externally via a token-protected cron
  endpoint. Liveness and readiness endpoints exist for Kubernetes probes.

## 7. Open questions and known gaps

- The README quick-start names auth variables the code does not read. The
  documented `docker run` example needs correcting.
- Feed categories have an icon field in the schema that no UI uses.
- No OPML import or export. This is a common expectation for a feed reader.
- The theme toggle is desktop-only; on mobile the system theme applies.
- Success metrics are not defined yet. Candidates: share of articles filtered
  versus restored, share of inbox articles dismissed without a summary, token
  cost per article.
